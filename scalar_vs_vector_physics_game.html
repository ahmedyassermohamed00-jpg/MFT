SCALAR QUANTITY

Magnitude + Unit (No Direction)

1D Value

"A scalar quantity is fully described by its magnitude (a numeric amount) and an appropriate physical unit, where applicable. It does not require direction."

Key Examples & Units

Mass (kg) Time (s) Temperature (K, °C) Distance (m) Speed (m/s) Energy & Work (J) Power (W) Density (kg/m³) Pressure (Pa)
→

VECTOR QUANTITY

Magnitude + Unit + Direction

Directional

"A vector quantity requires both magnitude and direction to be fully specified. Changing direction changes the vector, even if numerical magnitude stays constant."

Key Examples & Units

Displacement (m, East) Velocity (m/s, North) Acceleration (m/s², Down) Force (N) Weight (N, Downward) Momentum (kg·m/s) Torque (N·m)

HOW TO PLAY & CONTROLS

Quick guide to navigating challenges and rules.

1 Identify Magnitude vs Direction

If a quantity only has numerical magnitude and units (e.g. 50 kg, 300 K, 100 Joules), it is a SCALAR. If direction matters (e.g. 20 m/s North, 50 N downward), it is a VECTOR.

2 Controls & Mobile Touch

In Sorting Lab, drag items into containers or tap an item then tap the destination container. On desktop, use Keyboard Hotkeys: S for Scalar, V for Vector.
